## T2 — Every upgrade does what it says, every time it is taken

**D5 — Cause.** Two rules made picks change nothing visible. Scissors added `floor(level / 2)` (+1 at level 5), so picks 1 and 3 added no projectile. A weapon's stats were interpolated from level 1 to level 8 with counts rounded down (`levels()`), so the Laser Pointer's beams went 1,1,1,2,2,3,3,4: three of its seven level-ups added no beam and changed the rest by 5–15%. The level-up card showed only the weapon's general line.

**D6 — Weapons climb a ladder.** `levels(growth)` is replaced by `ladder(base, steps)`: level 1's stats and seven explicit steps, each adding to the level before. Every step changes something noticeable: one more of what it fires, one more cat passed through, or a stat at least 10% better (a unit test holds every level to that). Each weapon's level 8 is exactly its old level 8 (compared stat by stat before the tests), so the strongest weapons and the evolutions they lead to are unchanged; only the way up differs. Steps alternate between more of what it fires, homesickness and firing sooner, with size, speed or duration joining them.

**D7 — The card says exactly what.** `levelChanges(id, level)` reads the change from the two levels' stats and names each changed stat in one phrase ("+1 beam", "+40% homesickness", "fires 20% sooner", "+20% reach", "lasts 21% longer", "passes through 1 more cat"); `passiveChanges(id, level)` does the same from `modifiers`. `describeChoice` returns the joined phrases as `change`, shown on its own line on the card (`data-testid="choice-change"`). Because the text is computed from the numbers the game uses, card and effect cannot disagree; task 5's pause menu will reuse both functions. A new weapon's card keeps only its own line (it has no level before). Each weapon gains a `unit` (its count's word).

**D8 — Passives: the same step every pick.** Scissors now adds `+1` per level (+5 at level 5, was +3). The other passives were already linear; a unit test now holds every passive to an equal, non-zero step from its first pick.

**D9 — Balance.** Weapons are a little stronger on the way up (more of what they fire earlier) and Scissors at its top adds two more of everything. The seeded balance test ("positioning is the skill", `arena.test.ts`) and every arsenal and arena test pass unchanged; no assertion was adjusted.

**D10 — Review.** Approve, with three Low findings. (1) Scissors' card said "+1 of what each weapon fires", but the three vacuums (`pull`, `zone`, `gulp`) never read their count: the card now names what it adds to, derived from the weapons that use a count ("+1 beam, treat, droplet, ball, blade, piece or jump for each weapon that fires them"). Giving the vacuums a count meaning was the other option; it is a game-design change beyond the task, so not taken. (2) The e2e card check could pass with no card checked: it now counts the cards that name a change and requires at least one. (3) Not changed: a passive's card gives its constant step as a share of the base (Battery: 8 points of cooldown per pick, so its 5th pick is 11.8% sooner than its 4th), while a weapon's gives the change from its previous level. That is what "the same step each time" means; worth knowing for task 5's pause menu.

**D11 — `.next/dev/types` in the gate.** `tsconfig.json` includes `.next/dev/types/**/*.ts`, which `next dev` (started by the browser tests) writes and can leave half-written when the test server is stopped. Twice now it failed `tsc` and once `npm run build` with an error in `validator.ts` and nothing in the code. From here on the gate deletes `.next/dev/types` (this run's own generated output) before the type check and before the build. CI starts from a fresh checkout, so it never sees one.

## T3 — Sound settings in the lobby and the pause menu

**D12 — One component, the site's one setting.** `game-settings.tsx` holds Survival's settings (a "Settings" fieldset), rendered in the lobby (under the Keepers, on touch screens too) and in the pause menu (between its line and its buttons). Its Sound checkbox reads and writes `sounds.ts`'s existing setting through `subscribeSound`/`getSoundEnabled`/`setSoundEnabled`: default on, stored as `xenocats:sound`, unchanged. The arena's sound player already asks `getSoundEnabled` before every sound, so the switch takes effect at once; the dashboard's speaker shows the same state. Task 4 adds the aim choice to the same component.

**D13 — A checkbox, not a second speaker button.** A labelled checkbox in a fieldset fits a settings group, is reached with Tab and switched with Space, and reads as "Sound, checkbox, checked". Resume stays the button the pause menu focuses (the menu focuses its first `button`; the checkbox is an `input`). While paused the game handles only Escape, so Space reaches the checkbox.

**D14 — Acceptance criteria.** In the lobby and the pause menu, on desktop and touch: a Sound checkbox showing the site's sound setting; switching it in either place changes `xenocats:sound` at once and holds after Resume, in the other place and after a reload. All by command (two e2e tests).

**D15 — Review.** Approve, one Low finding, fixed: with sound off when a run starts, Start's `unlock()` creates no audio context; switching sound on in the pause menu then created it later, from the frame loop, outside any gesture, which a browser that needs one (Safari, iOS) would leave suspended for the rest of the run (the Survival page has no cat layer to unlock it on a later gesture). The checkbox now also calls `sharedSoundPlayer().unlock()` when it is switched on, inside the click or tap. Not observable in a test (no test can hear); checked by reading. Also noted by the reviewer, not changed: the fieldset is left-aligned inside the pause panel's centred text; a human should look at both places at phone width.

## T4 — Aim with a crosshair (desktop option)

**D16 — Which weapons aim.** Read from `arena.ts` `fire()`: the weapons that send something one way are the beam (Laser Pointer), chain (Laser Pointer Deluxe), web (Infinite Laser), spread (Cat Treats, Banquet), burst (Hairball), arc (Spray Bottle, Monsoon) and bounce (Yarn Ball, Yarn Apocalypse). Aimed, each goes towards the crosshair: the beams and treats fan round its way as they fan round a cat's (0.16 rad apart), the arc is centred on it instead of the way he faces, a yarn ball leaves within a quarter turn of it (instead of any way at all), the chain's first jump is to the cat nearest the crosshair within its reach, and the web's beams go to the cats nearest the crosshair. Aimed, the beam, spread and burst fire on their cooldown cat or no cat (the player chose the way); without the crosshair they wait for a cat as before. Never aimed, as the plan says: the vacuums (Vacuum Cleaner `pull`, Thunderous Vacuum `zone`, Forbidden Catnip Vacuum `gulp`) and the weapons that circle him (Can Opener, Bottomless Saucer, `orbit`). A unit test runs those three kinds with and without a crosshair and requires identical runs.

**D17 — Shape.** The simulation stays pure: `arena.aimAt(point | null)` sets player 1's aim point in the arena; with null, everything is as before (the same random draws, so seeded runs are unchanged). The view keeps the mouse's screen position while a run lasts and, each frame before stepping, turns it into an arena point under the camera (centre plus offset times zoom, so co-op's zoom-out is respected), and draws a crosshair there on the canvas; the system cursor is hidden over the play area while playing with the crosshair. Until the mouse first moves in a run there is no point, and the weapons aim themselves. Co-op: the crosshair is player 1's (WASD and the mouse); player 2's weapons aim themselves.

**D18 — The setting.** "Aim: Automatic / Crosshair" radios in task 3's Settings, on a computer only (`touch` false), in the lobby and the pause menu; stored as `xenocats:survival:v1:aim` (`arena-storage.ts`, anything unreadable is automatic, kept in memory if storage is blocked), automatic by default. The play area exposes `data-aim` and `data-crosshair` ("x,y" on the screen) for the tests, like its other `data-` attributes.

**D19 — Acceptance criteria.** By command: the option on desktop and not on touch, in the lobby and the pause menu; with it on, the crosshair follows the mouse (e2e); every aiming weapon's shot heads for the crosshair (unit, per weapon kind, including the fan widths), the exceptions unchanged (unit), switching back to automatic restores the old behaviour (unit and e2e), the choice kept after a reload (e2e). By reading only: how the crosshair looks.

**D20 — Review.** Request changes, three findings, all fixed; re-review approve. (1) Medium: aimed, the Infinite Laser's web took the cats within its area of the crosshair, which its beams from the Keeper (as long as that area) could not reach: it drew its web over cats it did not hurt; and the chain's first jump had no reach limit from him. Now both take, of the cats he can reach (the same reach test as automatic aiming), those nearest the crosshair (`nearestAimed`). A unit test checks every firing of both: nothing reached past his reach, and no reachable cat left out is nearer the crosshair than those taken; it fails on the old web targeting (a cat 805 px away against a 552 px reach, observed before restoring the fix). (2) Low: the mouse position carried over to the next run; the run's effect now clears it when a run starts, so a new run has no crosshair until the mouse moves (D17). (3) Low: the "exceptions unchanged" test now covers the Forbidden Catnip Vacuum and the Bottomless Saucer too (five weapons, four kinds). D16's "fan" rule for the chain and web is replaced by this.

## T5 — The pause menu shows the run so far

**D21 — Built from the run and the cards' own functions.** `arsenal.ts` gains `loadout(weapons, passives)`: each weapon with its level of its highest, marked when evolved, and what its next level adds (`levelChanges`, the level-up card's words); each passive with its level and what it gives in all (`passiveTotal`, from `modifiers`, in the cards' words); the free weapon and passive slots; and every evolution started (its weapon or its passive held) and not yet made, with what is missing ("the Laser Pointer at level 8 (now 5)", "the Battery") or "ready: the next chest evolves it", agreeing with the chest's rule (`evolutionFor`). A secret evolution (the Bottomless Saucer) is never named, as the codex keeps it until found. `pause-summary.tsx` renders it for each Keeper (co-op: under "Player 1" and "Player 2") with the run's time, level and experience, Resolve and cats sent home.

**D22 — A snapshot at the pause.** `show('paused')` stores the arena's state at that moment (`pausedState`), so the menu is read from React state, not from a ref during render; the run cannot change while paused.

**D23 — Phone and screen readers.** The panel is at most the screen's height less a margin and scrolls inside itself (`max-h-[calc(100dvh-2rem)] overflow-y-auto`); Resume stays the first button, which the menu focuses. Weapons, passives and evolutions are lists under headings; the run's numbers a description list.

**D24 — Acceptance criteria.** By command: the summary's content for given loadouts, evolution hints, the secret one hidden (unit); after a level-up on desktop, every weapon held listed at its level with "Weapons N of 6" and the passive slots, time and level shown, Resume focused (e2e); on a phone the summary shows and Resume can be scrolled into view (e2e). By reading only: co-op's two sections (no co-op e2e for the menu), and how it looks.

**D25 — Review.** Request changes, five findings, all fixed. (1) Medium: the desktop e2e compared the menu (a snapshot at the pause) with the HUD's `data-weapons`, which trails the run by up to 150 ms and is not updated while choosing, so it could disagree by timing; both e2e tests now record the card actually taken (kind, id, level) and require its row at that level or higher, with the slot headings agreeing with the rows listed. (2) Low: hints could contradict the game: with two evolutions ready a chest evolves only `evolutionFor`'s first, so only that one is "ready" and the other "ready, after the X"; an evolution whose missing piece has no free slot can never come and is no longer listed; the wording says "the next chest he opens" (in co-op, the opener evolves). Unit tests for each, the first against `evolutionFor`. (3) Low: Resolve shows "down, back in N s" for a downed Keeper, as the HUD does, and is labelled "Player n" in co-op. (4) Low: headings: alone, the loadout has its own "What he carries" heading, not under "The run"; in co-op each section is labelled by its own heading (`aria-labelledby`), not a second label. (5) Low: a unit test builds the loadout from a seeded arena's state after three level-ups; the touch e2e now takes a level-up first. D24's acceptance by command now includes the touch level-up; co-op and how the menu looks remain by reading only.

**D26 — Re-review.** Approve; all five findings confirmed fixed. One Low, fixed: the seeded-run unit test now also checks its evolution hints (each listed one has a held piece; only `evolutionFor`'s pick is ready) and that three choices made exactly three more levels (`1 + choices`, was `> 1`). Not added: a test for a missing weapon with no free weapon slot (the mirror of the tested passive case).
